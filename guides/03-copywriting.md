# Copywriting

Use these formulas when writing headlines. Examples are illustrative and invented; adapt them to the brand and language in the brief.

## 1. Tone: three voices

Short. Clear. Addressed to the reader. Never praises itself; says what the reader gains.

**This:** "What your work is really worth", "Double your margin", "Your crop's insurance"
**Not this:** "As the sector's leading brand, we offer our customers the highest quality service"

Rule: in a headline the brand does not describe itself. It describes a change in the reader's life.

The type branch sets the voice (see [02-visual-dna.md](02-visual-dna.md)). One brand's whole series stays in one voice, and two voices never mix in one post.

| Voice | Branch | Style | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Energetic** | Sans | Exclamation marks, imperatives, verbs. Focused on sales and benefit | *Brush, Shine, Smile!* / *Order Today* |
| **Quiet** | Serif | Ends with a period, no exclamation marks. Concepts over verbs. Promises without shouting | *Quiet Luxury That Glows in the Dark.* / *Comfort, Without the Show* |
| **Street** | Hybrid | Fragments ending in periods. Sensory words. Slogan rhythm. Two words can be a line | *Crunch Takes No Holiday.* / *No Faces. Just Flavor.* |

## 2. Headline formulas

Nearly every strong headline fits one of these sixteen. Name the one you used.

| # | Formula | How it works | Examples | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F1 | **Contrast** | Breaks an expectation and sets a new frame. The strongest pattern | *Less Chemistry, More Yield* | `Not [old way], [new way] [verb]!` |
| F2 | **Size clash** | Small and big collide in one sentence. Emotional and memorable | *Tiny Teeth, Huge Smiles!* | `[Small] [noun], [big] [noun]!` |
| F3 | **Triple command** | Three verbs make rhythm and ease action | *Plant, Grow, Earn!* | `[Verb], [verb], [verb]!` |
| F4 | **Metaphor claim** | Ties an abstract benefit to a familiar thing | *Your Crop's Insurance Lies Underground* | `[Benefit] = [familiar concept]` |
| F5 | **Outcome promise** | Says what ends or arrives. Needs evidence | *A Harvest With No Residue* | `[Unwanted]-free, [wanted result]!` |
| F6 | **Curiosity question** | For information posts and carousel covers only. The answer is inside | *What Isn't In Here?* | `[Question the deck answers]?` |
| F7 | **Ownership** | Puts the reader at the center through "your" | *What Your Work Is Really Worth* | `Your [noun], [result]` |
| F8 | **Season or urgency** | For campaigns, countdowns and seasons. Must be true | *Eight Days Left This Season* | `[Time], [offer]` |
| F9 | **Twin phrases** | Two adjective-noun pairs side by side, no verb. Very common | *Steady Trust, Natural Beauty* | `[Adjective] [noun], [adjective] [noun]` |
| F10 | **Colon setup** | The first part names the situation, the second gives the fix | *No More Site Chaos: One Team, One Plan* | `[Situation]: [solution]` |
| F11 | **Brand rhyme** | The brand name joins the rhythm. The shortest route to memory | *No Panic, [Brand] Is Here* | `No [problem], [brand] instead` |
| F12 | **Question and answer** | Line one asks, line two answers with the brand | *Drowning in Data? We Sort It.* | `[Problem]? [Answer].` |
| F13 | **Journey** | States start and finish in one line. For courses and processes | *From Zero to Expert* | `From [start] to [finish]` |
| F14 | **Exchange** | One-to-one equivalence. For donation and value messages | *One Well, A Thousand Lives* | `One [given], one [received]` |
| F15 | **Refusal** | Says what is absent to show what is present. Strong in hybrid | *No Frills. Just Work.* | `No [unwanted]. [Wanted].` |
| F16 | **Sensory continuity** | Personifies a product trait | *Flavor Doesn't Clock Out* | `[Trait] doesn't [human habit]` |

## 3. Rhythm

- **Split in two.** Nearly every headline breaks at a comma: part one sets up, part two lands.
- **Emphasis goes in part two.** Bold weight and the accent color belong there.
- **Balance the syllables.** *Tiny Teeth, Huge Smiles* works. *Tiny Teeth, Huge Smiles For Your Children's Oral Health* does not.
- **Look for repeated sounds.** Alliteration and repeated openers carry rhythm.
- **Seven words at most.** If you are counting, it is too long.

## 4. Copy layers

A post has four layers. Not all are needed, but the order is fixed.

- **Eyebrow:** 2-4 words of context such as place, period or category.
- **Headline:** 3-7 words on two lines, using a formula above.
- **Supporting line:** one sentence of at most 12 words that makes the claim concrete.
- **CTA:** 2-5 imperative words, plus a direction arrow in full-design images.

Editorial limits are eight words on a carousel cover and 25 on any other slide.

## 5. Call-to-action library

| Goal | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Discovery | Discover the hidden strength / See the difference / Browse the series |
| Purchase | Order today / Try it now / Get yours |
| Information | Details on our profile / Ask us / Free consultation |
| Contact | Message us / Order line: 0000 000 00 00 |
| Website | www.yourbrand.com, in a pill with a search icon |
| Educational carousel | Save this checklist / Send this to your team / Try step one today |

No overstatement in a CTA: "Don't miss out!!!", "Last chance!!!" and "100% guaranteed" are out. Use one primary action per carousel, and do not use empty comment bait.

## 6. Caption

Do not repeat the image. Say what the image does not.

1. A hook sentence, one line, from a different angle than the headline
2. A blank line
3. A body of 2-4 short sentences on the benefit or how it is used
4. A blank line
5. A one-line call to action
6. Hashtags

**Hashtag mix:** a few brand or product tags, then category and location tags. Keep only relevant ones and check current platform limits; the engine warns above five unless the brand's `voice.max_hashtags` says otherwise.

```text
The bacteria in your soil may work harder than your fertilizer budget.

This product turns nitrogen from the air into a form plants can use.
Less chemical fertilizer, better-fed plants.
It is applied with drip irrigation, so no extra equipment is needed.

For orders and dealerships, see the link on our profile.

#soilhealth #farming #irrigation #harvest #agritech
```

A claim like the first line needs evidence from the brand before it runs.

## 7. Language checks

Check spelling, punctuation, names and numbers in the language of the brief, and never strip diacritics to make a font work. For Turkish copy, also check:

- **de/da:** attached when it marks location (*tarlada*), separate when it means "also" (*sen de gel*).
- **ki:** separate as a conjunction (*öyle bir ürün ki*), attached as a suffix (*sabahki*).
- **Apostrophes:** before case endings on proper names (*Bursa'dan*, *Ramazan'ın*).
- **Circumflex:** *kâr* (profit) differs from *kar* (snow).
- **Capitals:** i capitalizes to İ and ı to I. Headline Case is a design choice, but sentence rules still hold.
- **Numbers:** 1.000.000 with periods, %25 with the sign first, 24 Ay with a space before the unit.

## 8. Banned filler

These carry no information: *industry leader, quality service, customer-focused, innovative solutions, best price guaranteed, unique experience, world-class, here for you, making a difference, one step ahead.*

Use a specific number, result or time instead. Not "a formula that makes a difference", but "twice the root volume in eight weeks", and only when that number has a source. Never invent statistics or testimonials.
