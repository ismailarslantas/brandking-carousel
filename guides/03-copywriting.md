# Copywriting guide

Write short, concrete sentences. Address the reader and explain a useful change in their life. Avoid corporate self-praise and filler such as “industry-leading,” “innovative solutions,” “world-class,” “unparalleled experience” and “100% guaranteed.” A specific claim still needs evidence.

Choose one voice: energetic sans, quiet editorial serif, or concise sensory/retro hybrid. Keep the voice consistent across the carousel. Use exclamation marks sparingly; quiet premium work should not shout.

## Headline devices

Use an audience-specific problem, small-to-large contrast, a three-verb rhythm, concrete metaphor, supported outcome, curiosity question answered inside, ownership/effort, genuine seasonality, paired concepts, setup with a colon, a memorable brand rhythm, problem-to-solution progression, journey, value exchange, or sensory continuity. These are creative options, not mandatory formulas. Do not use false urgency or unsupported outcome numbers.

Aim for 3–7 headline words. Build a natural two-part rhythm where helpful; avoid padding to satisfy a formula. A curiosity gap must pay off in the carousel. No fabricated statistics or testimonials.

## Layers

Eyebrow: 2–4 words that give context. Headline: one clear thought. Supporting sentence: normally no more than 12 words. CTA: one action, normally 2–5 words. Not every layer is needed on every slide. Total editorial limits are eight words on the cover and 25 on other slides.

Useful actions: save a checklist, compare options, try a step, read the guide, view the product, or contact the brand. Choose one primary action for the closing slide. Avoid repetitive sales CTAs in the middle of an educational sequence and empty comment bait.

## Caption and accessibility

Caption: a distinct opening angle, 2–4 short supporting sentences and one clear CTA. Add only relevant hashtags that comply with current publishing limits; do not treat a suggested hashtag count as a platform requirement. Do not simply repeat the cover.

Write useful per-slide alt text describing the information and relevant visual relationship. Verify spelling, punctuation, proper names and numbers. Repository examples and instructions are English. If a client explicitly requests another language, verify glyphs and language-specific casing; never remove diacritics to make a font work.
