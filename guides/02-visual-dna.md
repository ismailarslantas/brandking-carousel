# Visual style DNA

## Canvas and safe areas

1080×1350 portrait. Keep text at least 80 px from edges; 96 px is preferred. Top-heading compositions typically use y=100–520; hero areas y=380–1080; lower cards y=760–1150. Footer sits at y=1180–1300, while actual text stays inside the global bottom safe boundary. Micro footer text is normally 24–28 px. Important conditions must remain readable; never hide required disclosures in illegible type.

## Footer locks

A: centered logo for products. B: logo left/contact right for services. C: two-brand lock plus contact for partnerships. D: fixed three-part strip for direct sales or applications. E: top symbol plus bottom contact strip for template systems. Select one per series and preserve coordinates and scale.

## Typography

Sans: geometric or humanist; approachable, energetic, useful for technology, education, food and services. Serif: editorial prestige for luxury, hospitality, art and premium services; sans supporting text is valid. Hybrid: 1–3 script words above condensed capitals; not on the same line, with only one line tilted.

Reference scale: eyebrow 34–44 px; main headings 76–130; oversized type 160–320; body 42–56; cards 30–46; CTA 36–46; numeric highlights 90–160; footer 24–28. Keep the hierarchy stable. The engine uses a narrower set through its houses; expand layouts deliberately rather than forcing unsupported sizes.

Use two weights for hierarchy, tight but readable headline spacing and a single emphasized word or phrase. Huge capitals can tilt; small light text should not. Preserve punctuation and language-specific casing. Never guess a font family with certainty from a screenshot.

## Palette roles

Use a dominant field, a single accent and readable text, roughly 70/20/10. Neutrals provide breathing room. Rotate roles within the brand palette:

| Role | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| R1 | Solid primary background |
| R2 | Photo-led with a brand card |
| R3 | Light neutral with primary text |
| R4 | Dark neutral with one accent |
| R5 | Brand-tinted duotone photo |
| R6 | Approved secondary background |
| R7 | Two-tone gradient in one color family |
| R8 | Quiet tone-on-tone pattern |
| R9 | Warm neutral field |

Normally use at least four roles in a series and avoid adjacent repetition. This does not mean inventing four additional brand colors. Strict brand restrictions override generic rotation targets; record the exception. The engine implements solid, light, dark and warm, plus full pictures through the `photo` layout.

## Photography and texture

Use soft studio light, golden hour or blue hour when appropriate. Keep a deliberately calm text region. Product photos stay sharp and grounded; preserve full packaging and labels. Natural, context-appropriate people are preferable to generic stock poses. Cutouts need clean edges without halos. Paper, wood, soil, concrete and stone should support the message rather than add noise.

Do not darken every photo to make text readable. Use a clear panel, open space or another layout. One subtle pattern is enough. Use two related tones rather than three-color gradients.
