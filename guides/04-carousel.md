# Grid, carousel and story playbook

## Part A: The nine-post grid

Nine posts work in two places at once: one by one in the feed, and together on the profile. Build for both.

### A1. Variety, not a template

**Nine posts never share one structure.** No fixed color order, no repeating composition, no "every third post is like this". The grid's elegance comes from one palette taking a different role in each cell.

Three things stay fixed: the **footer lock**, the **accent color** and the **type branch and scale** (headline size within ±10%). A series strip footer stays pixel-identical across all nine.

Everything else changes: field color, palette rotation, composition, signature tool, photo tone, copy length and temperature.

### A2. Distribute the rotations

Spread the rotations from [02-visual-dna.md](02-visual-dna.md) across the nine posts.

- Use at least **four rotations**.
- The same rotation never comes **twice in a row**.
- The same rotation never sits **side by side or stacked** in the grid.
- The same composition appears **at most twice**.

Example, a red and cream housing brand:

┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
│ R5 duotone   │ R3 light     │ R4 dark      │
│ prop scene   │ layered photo│ magnifier    │
├──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
│ R2 photo     │ R1 solid     │ R3 light     │
│ door frame   │ blob info    │ Venn circles │
├──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
│ R4 dark      │ R2 photo     │ R6 secondary │
│ frosted card │ feature strip│ giant type   │
└──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
```

Example, a purple gradient education software brand:

└──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
```

Neither is a template. Redistribute for each brand. The eye should find something new in every cell, and all nine should read as one brand within a second.

### A3. The one exception: color-coded product series

The variety rule does not apply when a series presents the variants of a product range, such as menu flavors, collection colors or pack sizes. Composition, type scale and product angle stay **identical**; only the field color and product name change. The grid reads like a color palette, and the repetition is the system.

- All colors share one saturation level, with none left pale.
- A tone-on-tone pattern is the same in each cell, only recolored.
- The series fills the whole grid or a clear block, such as the bottom row. It does not mix in.
- When it ends, normal variety returns.

In a plan, declare this with `"product_variant_exception": true`.

### A4. Content roles

Not all nine posts sell.

| # | Role | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Manifesto or opening | Brand claim |
| 2 | Main product or project | Introduce |
| 3 | Concrete benefit | Gain |
| 4 | Information or education | Expertise |
| 5 | Conceptual | Interaction, saves |
| 6 | Proof or use | Trust |
| 7 | Objection handling | Remove doubt |
| 8 | Scope or portfolio | Show breadth |
| 9 | Call or campaign | Action |

The numbers are roles, not an order. Rearrange freely but keep the balance: **three information or concept, four product or benefit, two proof or call.**

### A5. Upload order differs from grid order

The newest upload appears first on a profile. To see the grid as planned, **upload in reverse order**, from 9 to 1, and say so at delivery. Pinned posts and later uploads shift the layout, and profile tiles crop to 3:4, so keep key content central.

## Part B: Carousel

### B1. Length and size

**5-8 slides**, with six as the sweet spot. All slides share one size, 1080 x 1350. Instagram crops to match when they differ.

### B2. No template here either

Slides do not flow in one repeated layout. Background, rotation and placement change from slide to slide. **Footer, accent and typography** hold them together.

The typical mistake is six slides of the same white card with the headline in the same place, which kills the feeling of swiping.

Instead: solid cover, photo-led context, light body, dark body, light body, solid close. Rhythm without repetition.

### B3. Slide roles

| Slide | Role | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: Cover** | Set the hook | Works alone as a post. A curiosity question or contrast formula. Needs a swipe cue |
| **2: Context** | Set up the problem | Makes the reader think "yes, that is my problem". Stands alone, since some readers meet it first |
| **3-6: Body** | One slide, one idea | One point, one visual |
| **Last: Close** | Action | Product, CTA, footer |

If the cover is weak, nobody sees the rest. Spend half your time on it, and compare at least three options with `brandking covers`.

### B4. Swipe cue

On every slide but the last, use one of:

- A right arrow in an accent circle at the vertical center of the right edge, 88 px across and 40 px in from the edge.
- A "1/6" counter in a pill at the top right.

The engine's counter and Crown Thread serve this purpose. Full-design images use the arrow or a pill.

### B5. Continuity devices

- **Fixed strip:** a 12 px accent line on the top or bottom edge.
- **Progress dots:** six dots at the top, with the active one filled.
- **Background family:** one color family, moving dark to light.
- **Edge overflow:** a graphic cut off at the right edge continues from the left on the next slide. Strong but demanding.
- **Prop continuity:** one object moves between slides: a door opens, a magnifier slides, a calendar page turns.
- **Crown Thread:** the engine's measured line across each seam, ending in a crown.

### B6. Ready-made flows

| Flow | Slides | Sequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product or project intro** | 6 | Contrast cover / Cost of the current way / Product and mechanism / Benefit 1 with a number / Benefit 2 with a number / Use and CTA |
| **Education** | 7 | Curiosity cover / The common belief / The truth / Why it matters / How to apply it / Common mistake / Brand and CTA |
| **Objection handling** | 5 | "What isn't in here?" / Three slides with one point each / Product family, trust signal, CTA |
| **Case study** | 6 | Result as giant type / Starting point / What was done / Before and after / The numbers / "You too" with CTA |
| **Campaign** | 5 | Offer in giant type / Scope / Conditions / Time limit / How to get it, with contact |
| **Project tour** | 7 | Location promise / Exterior / Shared areas / Interior / View and area / Feature strip / Price or plan and CTA |

Use real numbers, terms and dates. If a flow needs a number nobody has, change the flow.

### B7. Word dose

At most **25 words per slide** and **8 on the cover**. Details go in the caption, not on the slide.

## Part C: Story (1080 x 1920)

Do not enlarge a post. Rebuild it.

| Zone | Pixels | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dead zone, top | 0-250 | Empty: Instagram's interface |
| Breathing room | 250-400 | Empty |
| Headline | 400-800 | Same as the post, 15% larger |
| Visual or product | 700-1250 | Hero |
| CTA or sticker | 1250-1500 | Poll, question or link sticker |
| Footer | 1500-1650 | Logo |
| Dead zone, bottom | 1600-1920 | Empty: message bar |

Stories carry less: a headline, one line and a CTA. Supporting text and lists do not move over.
