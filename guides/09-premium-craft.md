# Premium craft: the creative director's standard

The bar is a case study you would be proud to publish on Behance: one idea, carried with discipline, down to the last pixel. Compliance with the rules is the floor. This guide is about what lifts work above it.

## Start with a concept, not a layout

Before any slide, write one sentence that answers: **what is the single visual idea of this deck?** Not a color or a style word, but an idea that could only belong to this brand and this message.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "Dark and elegant" | "Every slide is lit by one source, as if the bottle were the only light in the room" |
| "Bold and fun" | "The burger is treated like a boxing poster: loud type, one heavyweight in the frame" |
| "Clean and modern" | "Tabs pile up on the cover and clear away, one slide at a time, until a single card remains" |

Write it in the plan's `meta.concept`. If you cannot explain why the deck looks the way it does, it is decoration.

## Eight marks of premium work

1. **Extreme scale contrast.** Make the big thing much bigger and the small thing smaller. Timid middle sizes read as a template.
2. **Generous, deliberate space.** Empty space is a material. Cheap work fills the canvas; premium work trusts it.
3. **A restricted palette.** One field, one accent, one neutral per slide. The accent appears once and earns attention.
4. **Type as image.** Set the headline with care: tight leading, considered line breaks, no stranded word, never all weights at once. A good headline can carry a slide alone.
5. **One hero per frame.** One object, one gesture and one focal point. Everything else supports it.
6. **Real material and light.** Directional light, believable shadow, and texture such as paper, stone or skin. Avoid plastic gloss, flat stock lighting and the generic AI sheen.
7. **A system that varies.** The series is recognizable at a glance, yet no two frames share a composition.
8. **An edit.** Remove the weakest slide, word and element. What remains should feel inevitable.

## What makes work look cheap

- Centered everything, with equal sizes for everything
- Drop shadows, outlines, glows and bevels on type
- More than one accent, or rainbow gradients
- Clip-art icons or emoji used as icons
- A photo darkened so that text can sit on it
- Stock clichés, and generic blue-toned "corporate" scenes
- Small type crowded against a large empty box
- Fake 3D phone mockups and fake dashboards
- The same card repeated with the words changed
- AI artifacts: warped objects, melted edges, wrong hands, gibberish on labels

## Directing an image model

Brief it the way you would brief a photographer and a typographer.

- **Name the shot:** lens feel, camera height, light direction and time of day. "85mm feel, low angle, single window light from the left, deep falloff."
- **Name the material:** "brushed brass, raw linen, unglazed stoneware", never "luxury".
- **Name the negative space:** where it sits and how much. "The upper 40% stays empty for type."
- **Name the type setting:** family, weight, case, leading, alignment and exact placement.
- **Say what to avoid:** "no gradient background, no lens flare, no glossy plastic, no extra text".
- **Anchor the set:** after the cover is right, attach it to every later prompt. Then change only the composition, not the world.

Avoid hollow words such as *premium, stunning, beautiful, high-end, award-winning*. They add nothing a model can act on.

## The critic's test

Before delivery, look at the contact sheet from arm's length and ask:

1. **Concept:** can you state the visual idea in one sentence?
2. **Cover:** does it stop you at grid size, and is it clear who it is for?
3. **Hierarchy:** on every slide, is it obvious what you see first, second and third?
4. **Series:** with the logo covered, would you still know these belong together?
5. **Rhythm:** do the slides have loud and quiet moments, or is every slide the same volume?
6. **Detail:** at full size, are there artifacts, misspellings, stranded words or misaligned edges?
7. **Portfolio:** would you put this in a case study with your name on it?

If any answer is no, say so plainly and rework that slide. Do not deliver "good enough".

## Which path gives which result

The engine's layouts are clean, correct and fully measured, but they are flat by design. For portfolio-level work, use image-led slides for the cover and the key moments, and let the engine or a plate handle slides where exact copy and legibility matter most. A deck made only of engine layouts is a strong draft, not the final word in art direction.
